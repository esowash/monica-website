# Outreach to professional photographers

Goal: start a conversation, not close a sale. Monica sends these herself so they come from a real person.
Items in [brackets] are hers to fill in or decide. Nothing here promises a price, discount, or turnaround she hasn't chosen.

## Who to contact
Local portrait, wedding, family, real-estate, event, and product photographers who sell prints or albums.
Find them through Instagram and Facebook searches ("Ferndale", "Glen Burnie", "Anne Arundel" plus "photographer"), local Facebook groups, and the Google Maps "photographer" search. Start with 10 to 15, not 200.

## The offer
No free sample prints for now (decision: don't spend her materials yet). The hook is a conversation: invite them to send a file or a project and she'll quote it, rush jobs welcome. Revisit a sample-print offer once orders are coming in.

## Message 1: Instagram or Facebook DM (short)
> Hi [Name], I'm Monica, a print and design studio in Ferndale. I print archival-pigment photos on canvas, heavy photo paper, and vinyl, up to 2'x13', with finishing and mounting done in-house. I love your [specific work: e.g., "newborn series" / "marsh sunrise shots"]. If you ever need a local print partner, send me a file or a project and I'll quote it. I do rush jobs too. No pressure either way.

## Rules of thumb
- Name something specific from each photographer's work. A generic blast reads as spam.
- One follow-up only. Don't keep nudging.
- Send from her real account, and keep a simple log: name, date sent, reply, next step.
- Respect any "no thanks". Don't contact people who ask to be left alone.
- Once she has a few finished prints and a happy photographer, ask whether she can share a photo or a short quote, with permission.

## After a reply
Keep it simple: confirm format, size, material, and quantity by call or text. The website's call button is the funnel, and this message should end the same way.
